## The map

The force field is 11 km across, from Lansdown and the racecourse down to Wellow, and from Newton
Park to Bathford, with Combe Down in the middle, and a strip of it reaches out further north, up
Bath Road over Lansdown to Sir Bevil Grenville's monument. The city is built to the same detail as the
village (gardens, walls, doors, lamps, parked cars, shop signs) in squares about a kilometre across
that load in around you, and it all plays the same everywhere: traffic, naughty boys, cats,
pickups, buses, cars and balls on the pitches, in town just as in the village.

## Zones

The map doesn't open all at once. You start in the first zone round where everyone starts, and a
wall like the force field's, shimmering blue, stands round it: walk, drive, ride or fly into it and it
stops you, and says what opens the next part. The big map shades the zones still shut, draws the wall
in blue, and names every zone.

Each zone but the last has a strange structure of its own (see [Something strange](#something-strange))
with a few sockets, and one or two team challenges. Only the structure in the furthest zone open takes
objects; fill its sockets and the wall moves out to take in the next zone, and the camera flies over
it under a card with its name, its challenge and anything else it opens. Opening the last zone plays a
long circle over the whole map.

| Map | Zones |
|---|---|
| Bath | 1 Combe Down (the village's nine squares, out to Bath Spa), 2 Moorlands and East Twerton, 3 Sion Hill and Walcot, 4 Weston and Ensleigh, 5 Upper Weston and Batheaston, 6 Corston and Kelston |
| Cape Town | 1 City Centre, 2 Foreshore and Oranjezicht, 3 Waterfront and Green Point, 4 Clifton and Fresnaye |
| Bradford-on-Avon | 1 Bradford on Avon, 2 the rest of the valley |

Ways of getting about open with the zones too, and the card says when ("New: call a bus from any
stop"). Before then they're there but shut: a bus stop says "No buses out this way yet", a station's
train doors stay shut, the cable car's doors don't open, and the balloon crews and the boats won't take
you. In Bath the train to Bradford-on-Avon and the cable car are open from the start (both their ends
are in the first zone); calling a bus opens with zone 2, the bus routes with 3, canal boats with 4 and
the balloons with 5. Once open, travel still stops at the wall: a bus you drive, a boat or a balloon
can't go through it, and a bus you call only comes from inside it. Cars you can drive are always
there: they're parked in the first zone.

Put somewhere past the wall (a link with a spot in it, Go to, or the wall going up round where you
already were), you come to clear ground just inside it, the nearest there is, and the wall says why.

A room opens up to the furthest zone anyone in it has reached, and never goes backwards. To have
everything open, tick "Explore all of the map (no zones)" on the main menu, or add `?zones=0`; the
host's choice counts for everyone in the room.

Up on Bathampton Down stands Eric's oak, in memory of our friend Eric Benjamin Holmes. Down in
Twerton is Twerton Park, Bath City's ground: the old Main Stand with BATH CITY picked out in its
seats, the covered Popular Side, the big open Bristol End, the shallow Bath End, the dugouts,
hoardings and floodlights. You can climb the terraces and sit in the stand.

### Landmarks

Bath's great buildings are modelled by hand:

- **Bath Abbey**: the ladders of angels up the west front's turrets, the great windows, flying
  buttresses and pinnacles, and the tower. Go in through the west door to the nave under its fan
  vault, with the stained glass, pews, choir and organ, or [climb it](#climbing-bath-abbey).
- **Pulteney Bridge**: three arches over the Avon, Adam's front with its Venetian window and domed
  pavilions, the odd backs of the shops upstream, and the names of the shops there now. Pulteney
  Weir's horseshoe of white water is just below.
- **The Roman Baths**: in from the Abbey Churchyard through the Concert Room, under its glass dome,
  onto the terrace with its emperors, and down the stairs to the steaming green Great Bath (or
  jump in). The Pump Room's columned front, with "water is best" in Greek along its frieze, faces
  the Abbey.
- **Great Pulteney Street**: Baldwin's long Georgian street from Laura Place, both sides one
  design, block by block between the side streets.
- **The Holburne Museum** at its far end: the three-arched loggia you can walk up into, its
  portico and urns, and the glass extension in Sydney Gardens behind.
- **Argyle Street**, from the bridge to Laura Place, a shopfront to every house.
- **The Royal Crescent**: Wood the Younger's great half-ellipse of houses looking over its lawn,
  one front of giant Ionic columns all the way round, the round-headed window in the very middle,
  the balustrade, slate roofs and chimneys above, and every house a different depth behind ("Queen
  Anne fronts and Mary Anne backs"). Walk the flagged pavement past the area railings, cross the
  little bridges to the front doors, or get up on the roofs.
- **Grand Parade** along the river: Davis's colonnade and river wall, with a balustrade, urns and
  cast-iron lamps that light up after dark.
- **The Empire Hotel** on Orange Grove: its domed octagonal tower, and the roofline of a castle, a
  house and a cottage.
- **The Dundas Aqueduct** at Monkton Combe: Rennie's Bath-stone aqueduct carrying the canal over
  the Avon on one great arch between paired Doric pilasters, with the railway through its smaller
  arch, a deep cornice at the towpaths and balustrades at the ends. Walk the towpaths across it.
- **Sham Castle** on Bathampton Down: Ralph Allen's folly, all front, with an arch you can walk
  through. After dark it's floodlit gold from lamps on the grass in front, as it is for real.
- **Prior Park** above Combe Down: Allen's Palladian mansion, its giant portico looking down the
  valley, the steps up from the lawn, and its wings stepping down the hill.
- **Kingswood School** on Lansdown: Wilson's Tudor Gothic range with its tall entrance tower, and
  the chapel below.
- **The Bell Inn** on Walcot Street, the co-op pub: its red pub front under an 18th-century ashlar
  house, the rusticated doorway, the bell hanging over the pavement, and the old stables beside it
  with their faded painted sign. Walk in: the long bar with its hand pumps, settles, wood burner,
  piano and the little stage where the bands play, posters for Awful Sparks gigs on every wall; the games room with
  bar billiards, table football, pool and the launderette; then up the stairs at the back (or up
  the yard through the gates) to the garden, with the Pizza Bike, picnic tables under strings of
  lights, covered seating and the Back Bar with its own stage.
- **Sir Bevil Grenville's monument**, out on Lansdown where the battle was fought in 1643: a stone
  pedestal on steps with the Grenvilles' griffin on top.
- **The Skyline gate**, between Prior Park and Rainbow Wood, where the path out of the wood meets
  the top of the fields and Bath opens out below: two old stone gate piers either side of the path,
  one still holding its iron gate open, low estate railings along the field, a five-bar field gate,
  a kissing gate with a waymarker beside it, and a stump and a fallen trunk out in the grass.

### Streets, walls and bridges

Roads, paths, the canal and the railways cross on proper bridges, with Bath-stone parapets,
abutments, piers and arches, and whatever they cross running on underneath (the Dundas Aqueduct
carries the canal over the Avon and the railway). A bridge's deck runs straight from one bank to
the other, its road meeting it at its own height at each end and climbing to it along its own slope
(a side road joining it on the bridge meets the deck too), and where a square of the map's edge
splits a bridge it carries straight on, with no pier or parapet end in the middle. You walk on a
bridge exactly where it's drawn, right out to its parapets, and a slip road or approach beside it
never lets you through; and only a real bridge is built as one, so a path along the top of a
cutting has no parapets across the road. Over the canal every bridge leaves the water and the towpath open under an
arch springing from the banks, with no pier in the water, so the boats and you on the towpath go
straight under, its banks' walls stopping under the deck. Argyle Street runs level off Pulteney Bridge, kerb to kerb. Roads rise and
fall like roads, in cuttings and on embankments: evened out along their length, a side street
meeting a main road at the main road's own height, and none steeper than the hill it climbs (main
roads no steeper than 1 in 6.5 at all); a path or road crossing well below another goes under it on
a bridge, with headroom. The railway is laid as railways are, never steeper than 1 in 50, level
across its bridges and straight through its tunnels, and river banks slope down to the water.

Walls the lidar sees stand at their measured height, and streets are as wide as the walls either
side allow, with a pavement a kerb high only where there is one. Every garden has its own front
wall, gate and path to the door, with one fence or hedge between it and each neighbour. Jump at a
wall up to 2.6 m high and you pull yourself up onto it.

Nobody ends up inside a house. However fast you go (flat out downhill on a ride, or in a car, on a
slow phone), you can't pass through a wall, and the naughty boys, the cats and Bendy won't hop over
the back wall of a house dug into the hill and drop down inside it. If you're somehow shut in a
building anyway (shoved in by a car, a part of the map loading in round you, or dropping into a
courtyard off a roof), you're put straight out on the nearest clear ground, and so is anyone else.
Standing on a roof is fine.

### Day and night

Day turns to night and back every 24 minutes, the same for everyone since it comes from the clock.
At night the stars and moon come out, street lamps light the pavements, windows light up across
Bath, and traffic puts its headlights on (they come on in tunnels too), each headlamp throwing a beam
of its own. On a computer you can see
about 900 m by day and the lamps' glow from right across town at night; phones and iPads see a
little over half as far, to keep them smooth. The street lamps round you light the street: four of
them on a phone or iPad, and more on a computer the further its draw distance (it takes effect from
the next game), and when you're on the move, the lamps ahead of you light up first.

The watch on your wrist, bottom left above your health, tells the game's time: a black G-Shock
with the hours and minutes, the day of the week and the date (a new game day every 24 minutes).
After dark its backlight glows blue-green so you can still read it.

## Menus, settings and the debug panel

**The menus** are pages of BathText '91, a teletext service: the main menu is page 100, filling the
screen beside the crest of Bath, the pause menu is 101, Settings 200 (its pages 201 to 205), Link a device 300, the
wardrobe 400, the trophy cabinet 500 and the cheats 777 (type CHEATS), each with the time ticking over in its top corner. Green
buttons go on, red ones leave, and links to other pages are listed with their page numbers.

**Refreshing the page** puts you straight back in the game you were playing in that tab, where you
stood (up on a roof too), without the menu. Leave from the pause menu and the next visit starts at the menu.

**Full screen** is in the pause menu (Esc opens it and Esc again closes it, or II on a touch screen). In Chrome and Edge, Esc then
only pauses and you hold Esc to leave full screen; it stays full screen down the mines and in the
deli too. Safari and Firefox leave full screen when you press Esc.

**Every card and sheet** over the game has a × in its top right corner to put it away. The cards
that stop you to read them (a job's end card, a race's results, the opening poster of
a Wanted bounty, the night out's front page, the driving test's report) come up
as cuttings torn out of the Chronicle, each with the paper's masthead and the section it's from
along the top (Stop Press, Sport, Motoring, Wanted). The ones
that come up by themselves (the Chronicle's front page when you join, a job's end card, a race's
results, the opening poster of a Wanted bounty, the night out's front
page, the driving test's report, the Art Gallery's verdict, the fly-over when a zone opens) also
go on Esc (without leaving full screen in Chrome and Edge) or Options on a pad, and with a mouse a
click anywhere off the card puts it away and goes straight back to aiming. While one's up the mouse
is let go, so you can click its buttons. Leave it and it still goes by itself after a while.
Settings, the wardrobe, the trophy cabinet and linking a device have the × too, and Esc closes them.

**Settings**, on the main menu and in the pause menu, is remembered on that computer. It opens on a
list of its pages (Video, Controls, Controller once a pad's been seen, Play and Sound); pick one to
open it, and Back or Esc takes you back to the list:

- Video: draw distance (600 m to 6 km; squares more than about 850 m off are drawn simpler),
  resolution (Lowest, about 270 lines, through Normal, about 540, to Full; fewer if it runs slowly
  on a big or Retina screen), field of view (60 to 100 degrees), shadows, the retro look and the
  debug panel. Shadows go Off, Low, Medium, High or Ultra: how far from you they reach, as a share of
  the draw distance (a tenth, a quarter, half, or all of it), never further than 3 km (1 km on a phone
  or tablet), past which a house's shadow is smaller than a pixel. The page says how far that is at
  your draw distance. Close by they stay as sharp at every level; further off they're coarser. Trees
  cast them as far as they reach, even where they're drawn as flat pictures.
  Scenery detail goes Low, Medium, High, Ultra or Max: how far round you the grass grows (26 m to
  130 m) and how far off trees keep their full crowns (50 m to 250 m) and then their simpler ones
  (140 m to 650 m) before they turn to flat pictures. It changes on the spot; a phone or tablet
  starts on Low, a computer on Medium.
  Street lights go Low, Medium, High, Ultra or Max: how far off a lit street lamp still shows after
  dark, as a glow that's drawn bigger and brighter the higher you go so whole streets show from the
  drone high over town, and how many lamps round you light the street (two fewer than normal to six
  more). From Medium up, past those, every lamp's pool of light is painted onto the streets, gardens
  and walls round it, out to 1 km (Medium) or 2 to 3 km (High to Max), a little brighter far off
  than close to and showing through the night haze, so the town looks lit from the drone and from
  the hills; roofs stay dark. It changes on the spot; everyone starts on Medium.
- Controls: mouse sensitivity, and invert up and down and invert left and right for the mouse (which
  counts for a trackpad and the arrow keys too) and for touch. The mines' pause menu has the mouse ones.
- Play: passive mode (also a tickbox on the main menu), where cats, naughty boys, the teams, the
  Flag Lads, Bendy and seagull muck all leave you alone, even if you shoot them (other players can
  still shoot you, and traffic and trains can still run you over). The host's setting is the game's: everyone who
  joins plays by it, and if the host changes it mid-game it changes for everyone.
- Sound: music, sound effects, background (the traffic, birds and wind round you) and voice chat volumes.
- Reset to defaults puts them all back.

### Touch screens, and an iPad's keyboard

On a phone or an iPad you drag on the right of the screen to look, push the stick that comes up
under your thumb on the left to move, and tap to shoot; the buttons round the edge do the rest. A
keyboard works alongside (an iPad's Magic Keyboard, say): W A S D move, the arrow keys look round
(slowly at first, for a fine aim, then faster), Enter shoots (hold it for a burst) and / aims,
again to stop. The other keys do what they do on a computer.

On a touch screen everything has its own place, so nothing covers anything else. Pause is top
left, with the street you're on under it and then your job, the Flag Lads and the bus stacked
below that. A challenge's banner sits under the compass. Bottom left is a low strip with your health
and the time (the watch makes way for it), and bottom right your rounds or your speed. A boss's
health bar runs along the bottom between them. The buttons sit over the rounds: Fire, Jump and Aim
at the bottom, Crouch, Reload and Weapon beside them, Get in, Ride and the rest above them when you
can use them, and a top row of small ones for the binoculars, the torch, the radio and the view.

A trackpad or mouse on an iPad (the Magic Keyboard's, say) aims much as a Mac's does. Safari on an
iPad can't hand the pointer over to a game, so the pointer stays on the screen: move it over the game
to look round, and hold it near the edge of the screen to keep turning that way. A two-finger swipe
looks round as well, with no edge to stop it. A click shoots (hold it for a burst) and a two-finger
click aims, again to stop. The touch buttons go away while you use the trackpad and come back when
you touch the screen. ` (the key left of 1) turns the trackpad's aiming off, so it drags to look as
a finger does, and on again; the game remembers which. The mouse wheel's weapon change is Q or 1, 2
and 3 here, as the swipe looks.

### Game controllers

A PS5 pad (over Bluetooth or a cable), an Xbox pad or any other the browser sees plays the whole
game, with no mouse or keyboard. Press a button on it once the page is open so the browser sees it:
the game says "Controller connected" (and the Controls list on the menu says so too). A PS5 or PS4
pad works even in a browser that passes its buttons on in their own order; for a pad the game
doesn't know it guesses, and says the buttons may be muddled. F3 shows each pad the browser sees and
what's held on it. It shakes a little when you fire and more when you're hit.

Holding the right stick right over for a moment turns you faster, so you can swing round quickly and
still aim finely with small pushes. Once a pad's been seen, Settings (and the mines' pause menu) has
a Controller section: look speed side to side and up and down, invert up and down, invert left and right, the faster turn,
the stick's dead zone and rumble, all usable with the pad itself.

The buttons are laid out as Call of Duty: Warzone's defaults, so they feel familiar. Square reloads
on a tap and uses on a hold (about a third of a second); when there's something to use, get in or
take right there (the prompt is up), a tap uses it instead. Circle crouches on a tap and lies you
down on a hold, and either again stands you up.

One button does everything you interact with: use (E on the keys, Square on a pad, Use on a touch
screen) gets you in a car, opens a tin, takes the call or sells the tickets on Push the Bus, goes again after a race,
leans on a lock beam, cuffs, votes for a painting and the rest. Where two things are near, it does
the one the prompt names. Minigames that play quite differently (the tin lock, the bell chamber, the
Climb, the tightrope, the easel, the big map, the Driving Test's back seat) show their own controls
along the bottom while you're in them. Every prompt names the control for whatever you're playing
with: the key, the pad's button (as you've set it) or the touch button. They all come from one table
in the game's code (`web/src/controls.ts`), which the lists here follow.

You can move any button to another in Settings, Controller, Buttons: pick a row, then press the new
button. Whatever was on that button swaps to the one you left, and getting out of a car always keeps
a button. A button can carry one thing on a tap and another on a hold, as Square and Circle do.
Reset to default (Warzone layout) puts them all back. Buttons you moved before the Warzone layout
stay where you put them, and the rest take the new layout. While you play with the pad, the prompts
show the pad's buttons instead of keys, and say "hold □" where a hold is needed. A pad (or a touch
screen) aims without the mouse, so "Click to aim" only shows while you're on the mouse and keys.

**The debug panel** (F3, or Debug info in the pause menu on a phone or iPad) shows where you are
(game coordinates, latitude and longitude, OS grid reference, which way you face and which square
you're in), the frame rate and draw calls, whether the sound is running (and any sound that went
wrong and was cut off so the rest could still be heard), and how your link to each player is doing,
with their version beside their name. Shift+F3 or its Copy button copies your position, and O or Go to takes
east,north (or a copied link) and puts you there. With it open, T moves the clock on three hours
and Y moves it on a day, for everyone in your game (the day on your watch goes on through the week
and round again), and L steps the draw distance through 600 to 6000 m.

## Finding your way

**The minimap and the big map** (M, or tap the minimap) show Bath plainly, the same all over: grey
land, green parks and woods, blue water, bright streets with the main roads brightest, faint
footpaths and dark blocks for the buildings. The faster you go, the further out the minimap zooms,
so you can see where you're heading. The big map zooms with the wheel, + and −, or a pinch, and drags about. Its key says
what everything is; click a row to hide that kind of thing on all the maps, and again to bring it
back. The squares you've cleared of the Flag Lads' flags are washed faintly green, with a pint on
each pub you've won back, and a line in the map's corner says how much of Bath is yours; point at or tap
a square for what you've done there. Other players show as arrows in their colour, with a red ring while they're shooting; on the
minimap anyone out of range sits on the rim in their direction. Doors into the minigames are a
white star in a cyan ring, and the cable car is a red and white line.

**Markers**: click or tap a spot on the big map, or press G (or the middle mouse button) while looking at one, to put down your
marker in your colour. Everyone sees it on both maps, as a ring of light in the street and as a pin
on the compass strip with how far away it is. Do it again to move it, tap it again to take it down,
and it goes when you leave.

**Go to a pub**, in the pause menu, is a map of the whole force field with a pint on every pub in
it, about a hundred, drawn like the big map, with everyone's map markers and the other players on it. Point at one for its name, distance and food hygiene rating, and click it to
be on the pavement outside, facing the door. You can't go while dead or within five seconds of
being hurt, and once you've been to a pub, that's where you come back to life.

## Getting about

On foot, Space jumps. Hold it for the full height, or tap it for a smaller hop; you come down a
little quicker than you go up. A press just before you land, or just after you've stepped off an
edge, still counts. Walk head on into a fence, wall or car up to about your shoulders and you hop
it, and bumping into things slides you along them rather than knocking you back.

### The wingsuit

Stand anywhere at least 15 m above the ground round you (a roof, a tower, a high wall, a cliff
edge), or type SQUIRREL, and a wingsuit is yours for good, worn folded on your back (a little icon of it sits in the corner
over your rounds, lit while it's open). Jump off something high and press
Space (Jump) in mid-air to open it (it catches the air at once, like a hang glider), and Space again to close it and drop; landing folds it away.
It flies like Breath of the Wild's glider: A and D (the left stick sideways) bank it round in a wide
turn, W (stick up) noses down into a dive to pick up speed fast, and S (stick down) pulls up, swapping
speed for height. Pull up till you're too slow and it stalls, wobbling and sinking: push W to dive
out of it (let go and it comes round, but slowly and a long way lower). The view turns with you as
you bank. With nothing held it floats along level, sinking slowly, about ten
metres along for every metre down: off a 30 m roof that's nearly 300 m, and down a hillside it holds
you well clear of the slope, with W bringing you gently down to it. Keys
build up as you hold them, so a tap is a small correction. The mouse (the right stick, or dragging on
a touch screen) looks about freely without changing course, so you can shoot as you fly. Fly into a
wall fast, or dive into the ground, and it hurts. It won't open on a ladder, in a car, on a ride or
in a cabin, and the angel's wings win if you have both. Everyone else sees you spread out, arms and
legs wide with the fabric between.

### Rides

Rides turn up round the streets, pink on the minimap: a racing bike, a BMX, rollerblades, a
skateboard, a scooter, a pogo stick, and shopping trolleys outside the shops. Walk over one to have
it and press X (Ride) to get on or off.

A and D (or the left stick) steer, as in a car, and the mouse only looks about: leave it be for a
moment, or push on, and the view comes back round to the way you're going. Come off and you get back
on the way the ride was pointing, and A and D turn it while you're down. A pogo stick is the odd
one out: you bounce about on it as you walk, the way you look. W and S speed up and slow down, and
the hills are the whole game: downhill the wind
roars and the view widens, uphill it bleeds away. Space (Jump) does each ride's move: the racing
bike tucks, the BMX bunny hops, the skateboard ollies, the rollerblades jump, the scooter kicks for
a burst, the pogo stick bounces (time it on landing for a perfect one) and on a trolley you hop on
the back and hold on. Space in the air does a trick.

Come down near a kerb, wall, railing or parked car and you grind it; Shift (Trick) grinds one
you're alongside, or holds a manual on the flat. Airs, spins, tricks, grinds and manuals chain into
a combo, banked when you roll on the ground for a moment, and your bests on each ride are kept.
Come down sideways or mid-trick and you bail, back on in under two seconds. Someone pushing a
trolley can give a mate a lift: they walk up to the basket and press E to climb in.

Other riders are solid: you can't ride through them. Lean on someone riding alongside and you're
both shoved apart sideways, each turned a little off your line and a little slower; run into the
back of someone and they're pushed on a touch while you're held up. Each bump thuds, jolts the view
a little, kicks up dust (sparks, if it's hard) and rumbles a controller. Bumps are only between
riders: on foot, and in cars, nothing changes.

Speed traps (yellow boxes on poles, on the maps as yellow cameras) at the foot of Bath's steepest
streets clock you as you go past, however you're moving (on foot, on a ride, in a car or

tight as a real car does; take one too fast and the front tyres squeal and run wide, scrubbing off

#### Driving the bus

Take the wheel of a bus or minibus and you're on a route: the 2, as First really run it, a loop from
the bus station (out by Widcombe, Prior Park Road, Ralph Allen Drive, The Avenue and Church Road,
through Combe Down to Fox Hill and round Queen's Drive, then back by Bradford Road and North Road), or
the old 4 (the bus station, through town and up Lansdown Road to Lansdown, there and back), whichever
runs nearer, and round again. At the wheel of a
bus or a coach, as at a narrowboat's tiller, the naughty boys leave you be (your passengers they
don't). Inside, a Badgerline bus or minibus is fitted out as the real ones were: rows of green
moquette seats either side of the gangway, cream panels and pillars round the windows, lights along
the ceiling, yellow poles and rails with red bell pushes, and on the double-decker the stairs up
behind the cab. Everyone you pick up takes a seat of their own (a window seat on an empty one first)
and stays in it till their stop; look round from the driver's seat to see who's on. The route is a
green line on the minimap and the big map, with its stops along it and the next one ringed in
yellow; every bus stop round about is on the minimap anyway, as a little white disc. A card over the
speedo says where you're heading, the next stop and how far, and who's on.

People wait at the stops: pull in by one and stop, and they get on, each paying their fare at the
ticket machine. When someone's stop is next they ring the bell (ding ding, and BUS STOPPING lights
up), and you have to stop at the next stop: they get off with a "Cheers drive". Go straight past and
they complain from the back until you next stop the bus, anywhere at all, and then they get off and
tell you what they think of you (numpty is the kindest of it). Drive past people waiting and you
leave them standing. Get out and the shift's paused: back in the same bus within two minutes and it
carries on. The passengers are yours alone: anyone riding along sees the bus stop and go, but not who
gets on.

**A timetabled run.** At the bus station, a beacon on the pavement by the stands (Bus driver on the
maps) is where you take a job as a driver: press {use} there and you're at the wheel of a Badgerline bus
on the stand at the start of the route, facing the way it goes, sometimes with people already
waiting to get on. It's a timetabled run: out to the end of the route and back, calling at every
stop, to the timetable. (Get in a bus anywhere else, the station included, and it's the ordinary route.) The panel shows whether
you're on time, early or late (in real minutes and seconds: the timetable allows a steady pace
between stops and a little time at each), and you leave the station half a minute after you take the
job. Get to a stop early and you wait there: the panel counts down to when you're due away, and a
ding says it's time to go. You start with 100 points. Leaving a stop early costs points, so does
getting to one more than 15 seconds late (the later, the more), missing a stop altogether, and
getting back to the station late. Back at the station, what's left decides the prizes: 50 points
gets you a conductor's cash bag, 75 an inspector's whistle as well, and 90 an inspector's
gold-braided cap too (with the bus driver's cap if you left nobody at a stop). Once you've finished,
get out before starting another.

On Bathwick Street, outside No. 23 Chinese Kitchen, a BMW X5 has broken down, with a recovery
truck in front and its owner pacing the pavement, telling anyone who'll listen. He can't be hurt:
shoot him and he complains about that too.

### The canal

The Kennet and Avon Canal runs round the east of the map, from the Avon at Widcombe up the six
locks of the Widcombe flight, through Sydney Gardens' tunnels, past Bathampton and Claverton and
over the Dundas Aqueduct, with the Somerset Coal Canal's arm to Brassknocker Basin leaving it in a
wide basin at the end of the aqueduct, room to pass and turn round. The canal cuts
through the land as it was built to, whatever the hillside does: stone coping along the water's
edge, then a level bank on each side, the towpath on one of them, and a grass verge, or a retaining
wall where the ground beyond climbs into a cutting or drops away (above the railway at Bathwick),
down or up to the ground. The towpath stays level with the water's coping, going under the bridges
rather than up to them, and through both Sydney Gardens tunnels on a ledge beside the water: you
can walk through on it, and see from one portal to the other. Each tunnel is buried in its hill,
which closes over the arch and meets the top of each stone portal, with the cutting's walls
rising to meet it; the streets over the tunnels run over the top. Below each lock its stone walls run on past the bottom gates, and the towpath
climbs gently beside them on flagstones to the top of the chamber, the banks carrying on under the
lock's paving with no gap either side, at every lock (Bath Deep Lock too). The locks are Bath
stone, like the bridges; a footbridge over a lock's gates rests on its walls, and a path along a
lock runs down its paving beside the water. Woods along the canal stop at its banks, so nothing
hangs low over the water. The boats moored along the banks are where the survey saw boats
moored (the long rows at Bathampton, Darlington Wharf, Claverton and below Bathwick). A marina
off the canal, like Bradford-on-Avon's at Widbrook, opens on to it through its entrance as the map
has it, or through a channel cut in the bank where the map has none, with the canal's banks
stopping either side of the way in. The locks work:
press E (Use) at a balance beam and the gates swing, the water roars and the chamber fills or
empties. Narrowboats chug along, leaving a wake that spreads out to the banks, and wait at each lock. Hop aboard to ride on the roof, or press E
at the stern to take the tiller: W and S set the throttle, A and D put the tiller over, H or ↑ rings
the boat's little bell (Fire on a touch screen), and ← and → wave to people on the bank with your
left or right hand (◀ ▶ on a touch screen). It answers
slowly, so steer early and straighten early. Your view turns with the boat, and you can look about
with the mouse (it eases back to straight ahead, as in a car); C (View on a touch screen) goes from the
back deck to a chase camera high up behind, to see the whole boat, yourself at the tiller, and the cut ahead, and on
to the cinematic camera (towpath shots, bridges, the hills either side). Any boat moored by the bank is yours too: press E at
its stern to cast off and take the tiller (when nobody's about it goes back to its mooring). At the
tiller you're above it all: no naughty boy, team or Flag Lad will come for you or lay a finger on
you. Every
bridge clears a boat's roof; the steerer ducks under the low ones, and if you're stood up on the
roof a low bridge knocks you into the cut, so lie flat (Z) to go under.

### Hot air balloons

Early every morning five hot air balloons are filled on the lawn in Royal Victoria Park, west of
Marlborough Buildings: laid out from before dawn, filling from a quarter to five, and standing ready
from half past five till half past ten (game time, so about five real minutes in every 24), when
the ones left are packed away. A few more go up on their own flights soon after dawn and drift off
over the city. Walk up to a basket and press E to climb in. The first one in flies it, and up to
three friends can ride along.

There's no steering: you go where the wind takes you. **W** fires the burner and heats the air in
the envelope, **S** opens the vent at the top to let it out, and **A** and **D** turn the basket
(on a touch screen, push the stick up to burn, down to vent and sideways to turn). The heat takes a
few seconds to tell, so burn in short blasts and wait: let it cool and you sink. The wind blows
harder higher up and comes from a different way, so choosing your height is how you choose where
you go. The panel shows how high you are over the ground, how fast you're climbing or sinking, and
how hot the envelope is. C (View on a touch screen, Circle on a controller) goes from the basket to a
chase camera well back, to see the whole balloon with you in the basket (the mouse swings it round),
and on to the cinematic camera, which films you as the television does from a helicopter: long
lenses from a long way off, circling slowly or flying alongside a little above you so Bath fills the
picture behind, slow zooms in and out, and wide shots with the balloon small over the city. It tilts
down from a set piece to you drifting past it, or frames the two of you together, picks out another
balloon in the distance behind you, and at dawn and dusk puts the low sun behind you (by night, the
moon). Taking off and coming in to land it's down on the ground: wide across the lawn past the other
balloons standing ready, a camera rising on a crane beside you as you lift, the Royal Crescent behind.
You can't climb out till you're down again (anywhere: a field, a street, a roof), but from 15 m up E
jumps you over the side the way you're looking, in the wingsuit (a basket that high earns it, as any
roof does, and it goes on your back if it was in your wardrobe): Space opens it as you fall. Come down too hard and it hurts. A balloon left on its own comes down and is packed up where
it lands, and the crews have it back on the lawn before the next dawn. `?time=06:00` in the address
stops the clock in the morning, to try it.

## Jobs

Every game day a fresh set of jobs goes up on noticeboards at real places round Bath, a few near
the village and the rest across town, each a badge on the maps. Walk up to one and press E (Use) to
take it on. Everyone in the game is on the same job at once, with a card at the top left saying how
it's going, and the reward drops where it finishes. Its title card comes up over the crosshair for
four seconds, clear of that card and of the prompts under the crosshair, and anything the game has
to call out waits till it's gone. Everyone gets the same noticeboards that day,
and a job you've done stays ticked off till the next. Call off a job from the pause menu.

Lose a job or a team challenge and its result card has **Retry** and **Abort** in place of Continue.
Retry (R or Enter, □ on a pad, or tap it) takes it again in the same place, just as if from its
noticeboard: the entries open, and everyone who's in is put back at its start for the countdown.
Anyone else who was in it and still has the card up is entered too; Abort (Esc, Options on a pad,

you press Retry at once it starts the once, with you both in it. A lost pub takeover's card has

### The Flag Lads

The Flag Lads are out to cable-tie an England flag to every lamp post in Bath, whether Bath likes
it or not. They're made up, and the joke's on them (the flag-planting, the "taking our streets
back" from Swindon, mostly), not on the flag itself.

Where they're in charge the lamps fly the flag, with bunting once a square's mostly taken, and a
meter under the minimap shows how much of Bath is flagged and how many are down in your square. A
square flies 30 flags at most; shoot every one and it stays clear unless the squares next door fill
up. You can hear the nearest one flapping once you're close. Ladder lads in hi-vis come to put them
back up and run rather than fight; catch one and the ladders stop round there for a while.

Every few minutes the Flag Lads take over a pub. Get to the purple marker before the clock runs out
and clear them out, the Gaffer last, with bonus prizes inside 90 seconds. The crew grows with the
number of you, and with five or more they take two pubs at once. Each Gaffer has a silly name and a
trait or two (a gazebo that soaks up shots, a megaphone, a right-hand man, the whole WhatsApp
group), and he remembers: win a pub off you and he comes back tougher. A pub won back flies an ALL
WELCOME banner, leaves a ride outside, puts out free rounds and keeps the lads out of that square
for the day. Get the meter to 0% and Bath is flag-free till tomorrow.

The fight is at the pub's front, and it can be lost. Once the crew turn up, lads go up ladders
against the wall to hang their banner, and a bar across the top of the screen fills while any of
them is up there. Fill it and the pub is theirs. Shoot a lad off a ladder and the bar drops back a
quarter (the next one climbs up 15 seconds later), so you choose between the crew, the ladder and
the Gaffer. Alone the bar takes 150 seconds; each other player in the room makes it a fifth quicker,
and a bigger room puts more lads on ladders, rather than making any of them tougher.

At half his health the Gaffer runs: on foot, on a mobility scooter, or in his van, by what he
brought (a gazebo or the WhatsApp group means the van, a megaphone the scooter; alone it's never the
van). Wear him down (a fifth of his health left) or stay right on top of him and he puts his hands
up: cuff him (E) for bonus prizes. If he's 400 m from the pub, or has lost everyone, he's away, and
the next game day the Wanted poster on that pub's noticeboard is him: "the Gaffer, last seen leaving
the Crown in a hurry". The lads you left behind are still yours to clear for the pub.

### Wanted

A ringleader with a silly title ("the Duke of Disorder") hides out with his crew. The poster says
two things about how he looks, his routine (a pub, a park, a bus stop, round and round, but not
where) and two quirks: slippery, lookouts or mob-handed. A hidden third is given away by the first
clue. A search circle 600 m across goes on the map.

It opens with a big splash: the WANTED poster with his features as pictures (a tick for the two the
poster gives, a "?" for the one a witness will), his round of stops (where he's at now lit up), the
clock, and three lines on how to catch him. The job control (J, a hold of the pad's touchpad, or the
Poster button on a touch screen) brings the poster back any time, and it keeps up: a witness's word
on the third feature goes on it, ticked once they swear to it, crossed once they back down. The
job card names the features too, as little pictures, with how many clues you've found ("Clues 1/3")
and how big the circle is.

The circle is a red ring on the minimap and the big map, and in the streets a band of red light on
the ground with a low wall of light standing on it, so you can see its edge from inside, from
outside and from a roof. Outside it, the compass points to it. Clues stand under tall red beams with
a "?" over them, and go on your compass once you're within 150 m. Each clue found is called out
across the screen with the circle's new size and what was found.

He is not alone in looking like that: four to six of his crew are dressed like him, each with a
different mix of a hat, a coloured jacket, a bag, a dog and a limp (more of them the more of you
there are, and more lookouts). The poster's two features fit him and one or two of the others, and a
witness (E) gives a third feature that picks him out: but one witness in three is wrong, and owns up
if you press them (E again). Leaflets and knocked-out lookouts still shrink the circle. The first time
you see them, a call-out says how many men are dressed alike. Look at one (up close, or through
binoculars, B, from a roof or a hill if you can) and a card beside the crosshair shows his features,
each ticked or crossed against what's known, and what that adds up to ("Not him", "Fits the poster:
so do two more", "That's him!"). Then point him out with E. Point
at the wrong man and it costs you 15 seconds on the clock. Once he's pointed out, everyone gets a
marker on him. Put him down for the prizes, or wear him down until he gives up, cuff him (E) and
walk him to the police at the nearest bus stop or police box for more, while his mates try to
spring him. Cuff the wrong man and he complains, his solicitor is on the card, you lose 30 seconds
and the real one moves on.

He does something at each stop for a minute (a pint, feeding the ducks, waiting for a bus). Work
out where from the poster, wait for him there and catch him without a chase for a bonus prize. Each
bounty is six to eight minutes, with one second chance if he slips the net. A Gaffer who got away
from a takeover is a Wanted poster too, under his own name. It ends on a results card: how it went
(taken in, put down, ambushed or got away), the time, the clues, the lookalikes, the time lost to
wrong men, and with friends, who found what.

rematch. Each is a grey ! on the map.

- **The Flag Lads' banner** (a pub they kept): it hangs high up, held by cable ties, and two lads guard
  it. Shoot every tie (small targets) and it drops.
- **The smug ghost** (a race nobody finished): press E on the line and the ghost sets off along the
  streets through its gates. Get to the last one before it does, any way you like.
- **The tag** (a Wanted man who got away): hold fire, or the touch fire button, with the crosshair on the
  tag to spray over it, like a can. Cover 90% of the lettering.

A bigger room makes more to do rather than tougher enemies: more cable ties (4 alone, up to 8), ghost
gates (1, up to 4) and a bigger tag; only the banner's guards get a bit
tougher. Friends share progress, so you can split the ties or the paint. Calling a job
off leaves nothing.

### Races

From the noticeboard to a real place 1 to 3 km away, on a ride that suits the land in between.
At its noticeboard, E (Square on a pad) starts the race; the Chronicle pinned there too is a smaller
line underneath (N, or the pause menu on a pad). Taking a race opens the entries for 15 seconds:
anyone in the game, wherever they are in Bath, presses E to enter (whoever took it is in already),
and a big banner says who's in and counts down. On your own in the room there's nobody to wait for,
so it goes straight to the start. When the entries close, everyone who entered, and only them, is
put on a starting grid across the street with the ride, for a countdown to GO: a big 3, 2, 1, GO! in
the middle of the screen under three start lights, each with a beep, and a higher one for GO. The start
and the finish are always on a proper street, never on grass, and the route between them follows
the streets: it's drawn on the minimap and the big map, with a ring for each checkpoint still to
go. Checkpoints are gates across the road, and you have to ride through each one in order before
the finish counts; an arrow over the crosshair points at the next. Between them, any way you like:
finding the shortcuts is the skill. Each checkpoint is a split, flashing green or red against your
best, and the card shows where you're lying in the race. A clock counts down to the next
checkpoint, and what's left over carries on to the one after; it's set from bronze pace with time to
spare, more for the first leg, so a fair first go gets round. It turns amber with a beep when it's
getting short. Run out and you're out of the placings and the medals, but you can ride on to the
line for your time. Alone, a faint gold-pace rider sets off from the grid with you. Boost pads lie
along the road between checkpoints, two or three on most legs. Racers are solid to each other, as
all riders are, so you can lean on someone for the line; ride hard into another racer, going faster
than them, and they come off. Killed on the way, you're out of the
race: no time and no medal.

At the end comes a results card: your place in big podium colours, your time, your medal with a
shine on it (or DNF), your best on the route ("NEW BEST!" if you beat it), how far you were off
gold, and a few things from the run (top speed, boosts, slipstream, barges), with the podium and
everyone's standings in a room race (anyone who didn't finish at the bottom), any prize, and a
fanfare that suits it. It stays till you press Continue (Space, Cross on a pad, or tap it) or its
×, or for about 8 seconds; Again (use: E or □, or Backspace) goes again. On your own you get it as soon as
you're over the line or out. In a room race, over the line or out of it while others are still
riding, you wait where you stopped, held still and safe from harm (killed, you come back once it's
over), and watch the others from behind: Q and E or the arrow keys switch who (d-pad ← and →
on a pad; the arrows either side on a touch screen), over a banner with the standings so far.
The race ends when everyone's over the line or out of it, or a minute after the winner, and everyone
gets the results card together. There's gold, silver, bronze and a hidden author's time, and you
race ghosts of your best run and the room's fastest. Use (E or □; Again on a touch screen; Backspace too) puts
just you back on the line, once you're over it or on your ride. There's a daily
race round the village too, with a board of times and the local legend.

### Seagull Attack

Tourists on benches with sandwiches, and the gulls of Bath want them. Five waves come in off the
roofs: herring gulls that swoop (shoot them), hoverers that drop straight down (stare them out with
your crosshair), gangs of black-headed gulls (shout at them with E), walkers that sneak up behind,
and poopers that splatter your screen. The last wave brings Big Lad, a seagull the size of a bus,
who drops poo bombs and dives for whole sandwiches; his beak's the weak spot. The benches go out on
the nearest open ground, clear and flat all round (if it's tight by the noticeboard, the board says
how far and which way), and everyone starts in the middle of them, facing a tourist. A pin over each
tourist shows through walls and hedges, green to red as their sandwich goes, flashing while a gull's
after it; the ring is on the minimap. The tourists point out gulls on the way, wave their arms and
shoo, duck, shout for help if you're far off, cheer a good shot and storm off when their sandwich is
gone. Each wave comes in from one side, with a banner naming what's in it, and every kind looks
different: white herring gulls, slate-winged hoverers, a small dark gang coming in low and tight,
brown walkers on foot, fat yellowish poopers trailing drips. A hoverer hanging over a bench has a
ring of dark segments round it, and they light up one by one while your crosshair's on it: fill the
ring and it gives up (the ring is only there while staring works). A gull flying off with a bite
carries it in its beak, slowly, with flashing red brackets round it that show through roofs: shoot
it before it gets away and the bite's saved. A gull that's beaten (shot, stared out or shouted off)
bursts into feathers, squawks, tumbles and turns a see-through grey as it beats off over the roofs,
three times as fast as any thief; it can't be shot again, and nothing's left round it. Between
waves three crates go out and the team takes one. One is always a box of rounds,
enough for everyone for the next two waves (more for a harder poster), so you choose ammo or an
upgrade (chips as a decoy, a plastic owl, a brolly, a megaphone, second helpings): aim well and you
can skip the rounds. Stars for how much sandwich is left, a daily twist (bank holiday, Chip
Tuesday, a hen do, bin day), and Backspace for another go. Losing leaves no scar.

### The night out

Jobs lead into each other. Within the minute of something being done, a follow-up turns up nearby, as
a poster on the spot or a phone call with a marker. It is an ordinary job, built the same on every
screen, so everything about it works as usual and it scales with the room as that job does.

- **Pub won back:** the landlord says last one to the next pub buys. A race poster goes up at the pub,
  to the nearest other pub at least 250 m off.
- **Gaffer cuffed:** his phone has the Flag Lads' plan. You get a call and a purple marker on the
  square they are eyeing next, the most flagged one beside a square you have cleared or won back.
- **Seagull Attack won:** a tourist tells you there are men putting flags up, and a marker shows the
  ladder crew. A crew needs a lamp near enough, so now and then there is no tip.
- **Gaffer gets away:** nothing new; he is tomorrow's Wanted poster, and goes on the night's card.

You do not have to follow any of them. A poster comes down after 75 seconds and a call after 90, and
taking any job by hand (or the follow-up itself) takes the other posters down, so a follow-up never
gets in the way. Only one follow-up is up at a time.

**Last orders.** After three things done (jobs won, pubs won back, the Gaffer caught) and a quiet
moment, everyone is called to one pub: the one won back most tonight, or the one nearest the night's
first deed. Get the whole room within 30 m of the bar and the night's card comes up, a newspaper
front page written from what you did. If someone has wandered off, whoever came gets it when the
call runs out after four minutes. The next night starts from nothing. It works on a touch screen
too: the posters are taken with the usual button, and the card is just something to read.

## Always there

### Climbing Bath Abbey

No clock, just a hard way up for those who find it. One of the ladders of angels on the west
front's turrets can really be climbed (step onto the stone at its foot), all the way to the crown.
Then over the roofs and along the nave's ridge to the tower, up onto the ledge round it, a running
jump over the gap 27 m up, and up the stones jutting from the tower, round all three faces and over
the battlements. Fall and you land on whatever's below.

On top, a pair of angel's wings floats over the roof (and the first time, a halo for your
wardrobe). Walk into them and jump off: you glide, glowing gold, Space flaps you higher and looking
down dives, for 45 seconds. The wings come with a heavenly guitar: look up for the high notes, down
for the low ones, turn to shred, and everyone nearby hears your solo.

### The record player on Solsbury Hill

Up on the flat top of Little Solsbury Hill, over Batheaston, someone has left a portable record
player on a folding table, lid propped open and a record on it. It plays whatever MIDI files are in
the repo's `web/Record player/` folder, one after another, round and round, on synthesised
instruments with the crackle of an old record; with nothing in the folder it sits silent. You hear
it from about 70 m off, louder as you come up to the table, and the soundtrack fades down while
you're there. Between records the needle sits in the run-out groove for a few turns. Everyone up
there hears the same bit at the same time.

### Ball games

Pitches are marked out for their sport: football goals, rugby posts and cricket wickets. Running
tracks (Kingswood's on Lansdown, the university's on Claverton Down) are red, with their lanes and
the finish line marked, and an infield with nothing else on it is a rugby pitch. Now and
then a ball turns up on one (a white dot on the map): a penalty or a shot from the edge of the box,
a kick at goal from the tee, or a ball to bowl at the stumps. Walk up to it, A and D aim (a tap for a
fine nudge, hold to swing round quickly), W and S set the power, Space or a click kicks or bowls, and E walks off. Each ball has its own wind to
allow for. Score and a prize lands: Bucks Fizz, a sniper rifle, rounds, or Lucozade, which doubles
your damage for 30 seconds. Then the telly takes over, with replays in letterbox and slow motion.

A penalty is a set of three against the computer's keeper, who guesses a side, now and then reads
you, and leans towards the side you last put one. The aiming dots (and the wind figure) are only
there for the first kick; after that, read the wind from the flag on the pitch's corner. Two goals
from three wins the prize.

With friends it's a shoot-out. If others are near the goal when you line up a penalty, they're asked
for a keeper: stand on the goal line and press E (the Use button on touch) to go in goal. The keeper
dives by holding A or D and pressing Space (two Dive buttons on touch); a dive goes one way, takes
under half a second to stretch across the goal, and a dive after the ball is past can't save it. A
dive before the kick is a guess. A card at the top shows the score and whose turn it is, and saves
get the telly replay as well as goals. It scales with the number of players:

- 2 players: you swap, one kicking and one keeping; five kicks each, then sudden death.
- 3 or 4: two teams (alternate players in the order they joined) take turns, rotating kickers and
  keepers; five a side, then sudden death.
- 5 to 8: a queue. Everyone takes three kicks in turn and keeps goal for the next kicker; most
  goals wins, and a tie is shared.

The winners get the prize. If someone walks off or leaves, the shoot-out ends.

### Something strange

Now and then something turns up that doesn't belong in the town: a softly glowing, not-quite-round
lump. It comes only from the big things: a pub won back from the Flag Lads, a job done to gold or
three stars (a few of those a day at most), or a team challenge won. It appears where you did it.

E picks it up, and then it's in your hands instead of a ride or a ladder. You can't sprint with it,
and you're a little slower. X sets it down; get downed and it drops where you fell, and anyone can
pick it up. Take it to the structure, a leaning stack of dark frames with empty sockets up its
corners, standing on open ground in the furthest zone open (Firs Field in Combe Down to begin with),
and E sets it in the next socket. A structure that's full takes no more: take yours there and it
tells you where the next one is, how far and which way.

Both maps mark every structure in the zones open so far: green once it's full, red while it wants
filling, and the one taking objects now pulses with a white edge. The big map writes how full that
one is under it ("2 of 6"), and pointing at any of them names where it stands, its zone and how full it is. On the
minimap, structures a walk away sit on the rim their way, and the one taking objects sits there from
anywhere, with how far it is. The key switches them off and on. Structures in zones not open yet
aren't shown.

When a structure is full, the next [zone](#zones) opens:
the camera flies over to it and its name comes up (E, Space or a tap skips it), and its challenges
have noticeboards of their own from then on, every day. Everyone in a room sees the fly-overs, and a
room carries on from whoever has filled the most sockets on that map; it never goes backwards.

### Push the Bus

A team challenge: a broken-down bus to shove up the steepest stretch of street on the map, heaving
on the count (the README has the whole of it). Up the hill are bus stops with pensioners queueing
for it. Bring the front door up to a stop and slow down, and the queue shuffles over: one of you
lets go, goes to the front door and presses use to sell the tickets, while the others hold the bus
(on your own it's chocked at the stop while you sell). Each pensioner counts out the fare a coin at
a time under the crosshair: press use as it goes green, exactly right. Too soon and they start
again, too late and they want change. Each one aboard makes the bus a little heavier, and go past a
stop without selling and that queue shakes its sticks at you and writes to the Chronicle.

Those aboard get crosser while the bus is slow or rolling back. They lean out of the windows and
shout, ring the bell (the cab hears they want the next stop; not stopping there makes the queues
further up crosser), throw handbags, walking sticks, sherbet lemons and thermoses at the pushers
(a splat on your screen, and your next heave is a grade worse), walk off up the hill in a huff, or
start a row about the front seat that rocks the bus and weakens the heaves. Going well, they cheer,
and for the next heave the perfect window is wider; now and then a feisty one gets off and pushes.
The result card gives your time, a grade, and how happy the passengers were at the top.

### Get Wood

Kingswood's bonfire, up on Lansdown. The tarmac court on the north side of Fonthill Road, just
along from College Road (a badge on the maps), has the woods climbing the hill behind it, and
bundles of firewood lie about in there: some by the path that runs up through the trees, the rest
scattered among them. E picks one up; carry it down to the court and walk up to the pile and it
goes on. It takes 25 bundles to build the bonfire, and everyone in the game builds the same one.

There's no clock to beat: the day just goes by. Once the bonfire's built, the next time night falls
(straight away, if it's finished after dark) a crowd in lab coats walks over from Westwood with
sparklers. The first one there lights the bonfire, and once they're all round it they take turns to
bend down and light rockets, all night: you can see and hear them from across town, and each burst
lights up the trees below. (F3's debug panel has a Bonfire night button, N, that builds it and
moves the clock on to dark.) By morning it's burnt out, leaving a ring of ash, and a new pile is
started. Not finished by dark? Nothing's lost: what's on the pile stays there for tomorrow, even if
you leave and come back another time.

While there's wood to fetch, a pair of prefects in suits turns up behind anyone who comes near the
court and follows them about, telling them to hurry up, do more, and stop dawdling. They don't carry
any wood themselves.

### The Climb

A team challenge up a crag of its own, high over the cloud, which only counts when the whole team
stands on top together. Walk into the face (or E) to grab a hold; the keys or the stick reach for
the next, Space lunges and C lets go. Your grip drains as you hang and comes back on a ledge or a
big white jug. With a team a few stretches start blank, their first hold (big and orange) out
of reach from the ledge under it: a mate crouches (C) and you stand on their shoulders and jump for
it. There's one such stretch with two or three of you, two with four or five and three with six or
more, and with four or more one of them (a bigger red hold) needs two stacked: one crouches, the
next stands on their shoulders and crouches too, and the third climbs up on top. Alone there are
none. On a ledge, E by the lip hauls up a mate just below.

Ropes: the first one onto a ledge can drop its rope (E by the coil). With a team the rope only
holds while someone on the ledge holds it: E at its top takes the strain, and you stay put while
you do. A strain meter under the crosshair shows how many are hanging on it, and your grip drains
faster the more there are, so send them up one at a time. E (or C) lets go, and the rope goes slack:
anyone on it slides down to the ledge below and the rope lies in a heap there until someone up top
holds it again. Alone the rope is tied off and holds on its own.

The traverse: with a team there's a gap in its holds too wide for anyone to lunge across. Whoever
gets to the hold before it can stretch across (E) as a bridge: the others reach for the bridge
like a hold and climb over to the far side. The bridge's grip drains fast, so be quick. Once a
mate is hanging on the far side, the bridge reaches their way (D or the stick) and is pulled
across; E brings the bridge back to the near hold instead. Alone the traverse has no gap.

## Fighting and trouble

### Weapons and pickups

You start with a rifle and 120 spare rounds. 1, 2 and 3 pick your fists, the rifle and the sniper
rifle, and right click aims. The sniper rifle has 10-round magazines, a bolt and a scope that
sways. Pints (40 health) wait outside the nearest pubs, and sniper rifles, rounds and the odd pint
turn up outside shops and pubs near you. Run dry and it's fists. (The middle of Firs Field used to
fill with a sniper rifle or a bottle of Co-op Bucks Fizz, but it's in Combe Down CofE Primary's safe
zone, below, so it stays empty now.)

It's paintball by default: the rifle is a marker with a hopper of loose balls on top that roll and
rattle as you move and shoot. The sniper is a magazine-fed marker firing First Strike rounds, little
finned shells you can see stacked in its clear magazine; they fly flat and spinning where the
rifle's balls lob and wobble, crack instead of pop, and leave a tighter, longer splat. Reloading it
drops the empty magazine, slaps a full one in and works the bolt. To reload the rifle you bring up a pod, flip it open over the hopper
and pour the balls in; sprint before they're in and you keep only what you had. Each player's paint
is their own colour (the balls in your hopper too) and splats where it lands.
For real guns and blood, add `?gore=1` to the address (`?gore=0` to go back) or type `claret`
while playing. The browser remembers it, and it's only what you see.
Type `cheats` while playing for a page listing every cheat.

Press `\` (or Chat and commands in the pause menu, on a touch screen) to get your phone out: an old
Nokia's colour screen on the right, counting down the 160 letters a text can have. Type and press
Enter, and it goes to everyone in the game, in any town; texts from the others come in with the old
"SMS" beep, and the phone pops up to show them for a few seconds. Esc, Back or `\` put it away.

  stands in front of another; Blueprint, the same lines bright on dark shapes; and What's what, each kind

  buildings, set pieces, landmarks, walls and fences, plants, water, the canal's works, the railway

low lunge two or three metres forward that lands you flat with a thud, and a moment to get your
breath before you can get up or shoot. A wall stops the dive dead; you can't dive off a ride, in
water or carrying something strange. Down the mines, crouching (C) mid-sprint dives. F (Torch) switches your torch on, and everyone sees
the beam.

### Naughty boys

Gangs hang about in alleys, at bus stops and on street corners all over Bath, Fox Hill most of all,
and some arrive in a Nova. Get spotted and they chase you: faster than a walk, slower than a
sprint, and they give up if you get away or out of sight. If they catch you they hit you.

Nobody dies. Splat one enough and he goes over, then picks himself up and legs it, far faster than
you can run, shouting something on his way in a voice gone high with fright, and fades away once he's well off: he's had enough,
that's all. The same goes for the teams and the Flag Lads. Hit one with a car and he's sent
sprawling to the side, then gets up and runs off square to the way you were going.

The rugby and cricket teams turn out at their own pitches: one team when you walk on to a rugby
pitch or cricket field (and another when a friend joins you there), then another every three to
five minutes while anyone's still on it. They're bigger and quicker than naughty boys, and they
come looking for anyone near their pitch.

The lawn in front of the Royal Crescent, inside the ha-ha, is private: it's for the people who live
there and the guests of the hotel. Like the teams at their pitches, the residents are out on it,
standing about on the grass and on their doorsteps, whenever anyone's in sight of it: gentlemen in
tweed, blazers and morning coats with their umbrellas, ladies in long dresses, big hats and pearls
with their handbags, and now and then a hotel guest in his bathrobe and slippers. Step over the
fence on to the lawn and they come for you. They're the posh lot's answer to naughty boys, and they
tell you what they think of you all the way ("Get off the lawn!"). They only go for someone on the
lawn or right by it, give up once you're well off it with a parting word ("And stay off!"), and go
back to where they were standing. More come out every minute or so while anyone's still on it, and
they go back indoors once there's nobody about.

On Friday and Saturday nights (by your watch, from eight in the evening till three in the morning)
there's a night out at the top of Milsom Street, where it meets George Street: a crowd of naughty
boys and Naughty Girls (short skirts, going-out tops, heels and a handbag) stood about in twos and
threes. Every so often two of them kick off with each other, sometimes with a mate piling in, till
one has had enough and legs it. They leave you alone unless you get right in among them or shoot
one, and then only the few nearest join in. The girls slap rather than punch, and tell you what
they think of you ("Sorry love, washing me hair tonight!" more than anything). A girl will slap a
lad, but the lads never hit the girls: he just stands there and takes it. They come out of the pubs and restaurants round about (the restaurants till
eleven, the pubs all night) to keep the numbers up, and turn up already stood about if you arrive in
the middle of the night (by train, or straight into a pub). After midnight half of them drift down to
Mr D's chip van at the top of Old Bond Street and hang about there, and at three they all head back
to the pubs and off home. (`?nightout=1` in the address makes every night a
night out, to try it.)

### Safe zones

Every nursery and primary school has a safe zone: its grounds and 200 m round them. Nothing that
fights turns up in one. No gang of naughty boys hangs about there, no team turns out on a pitch
there, the Flag Lads never take over a pub within reach of one or send a ladder lad, and a Wanted
man never has a stop near one. Nothing to pick
up turns up there either, apart from rides and ladders: no markers, paintballs, pints or fizz, no
drops, and a prize won on a pitch in one is a ride. A chase can still run through one; nothing
just starts there. The schools come from the map's names: nurseries, pre-schools,
playgroups, infant, junior, primary, church and prep schools, and Three Ways School.

### Cats

Cats keep to the residential streets: big and small, the odd kitten or fat one, long-haired ones of
most breeds. Shoot one and the nearest come for you, with more trickling in. They're faster than
you, but get 80 m from where it started and they give up, and you get 30 seconds' grace after.
After dark they're braver: more are out, the calm ones sit round your feet, and more come for you.
Toot a car horn at them and the calm ones near it bolt, flat out away from you, for a few seconds
(a swarm that's after you takes no notice).

### Bendy

Up at the top of town, within 100 m of 1 Fonthill Road, lives Bendy: four metres of black rubbery
limbs with a pale grinning face and a lamp in his forehead. He only comes out at night, sweeping
his patch with his lamp like a lighthouse. Get caught in the beam and it goes red and locks on, and
he sinks into the ground and pops up beside you, again and again, so running doesn't help. He takes
over a hundred rounds (under fifty in the head), so bring friends; a roof is out of his reach. He
melts into a puddle of ink and leaves three of the best prizes, and a few minutes later he's back.

By day Bendy has another life, at an ordinary size: a history teacher on a school trip, at
Grenville's monument at the far end of the force field's strip up Bath Road (look for the Kingswood
School minibus parked on the lane, which you can drive). Go up to him and he tells you about the
Battle of Lansdown, a fact at a time. At dusk he sinks into the ground, and that's the lesson over.
Shooting him gets you nothing but a telling-off.

Just over the way, Jamie Mair has a crafty smoke on Lansdown: some of the time at the top of Van
Diemen's Lane, by Lansdown Road, and the rest in the bushes between Kingswood School's chapel and
Lansdown Road. Walk right up to him and he gives you one. While you smoke it, for a minute, nothing
can hurt you and every hit you land does double (you glow red, and the edges of your view throb).
He'll give you another a minute after it's out. Shooting him does nothing but annoy him.

## Minigames

The doors into them are points of interest on the maps, with a neon sign and a ring of light on the
step.

**Under Combe Down** is a co-op dungeon crawl in the stone mines under the village, through the
front door of 1 The Firs (`web/mines.html`). Pick a class, go down with your
party and fight monsters, mini-bosses and bosses in the dark. Every player generates the same level
from a shared seed.

**COMBE DOWN WITH ME** is a co-op cooking game in the kitchen of the Darling Deli, for one to four
cooks (`web/deli.html`). Go in under the orange neon sign. You all
work one three-minute shift: fetch from the crates, chop, fry and bake, plate up and hand it
through the pass. The menu is a Bath Bun, a Bacon Butty, a Ploughman's, a Cheese & Tomato Sarnie
and The Full Combe Down. Anything left on the heat burns, quick service tips better, and takings
earn up to three stars. WASD walk, Space or E picks up and puts down, and holding F or Shift chops.
Your mic is open in the kitchen.

## Playing together

Multiplayer is peer to peer over WebRTC: type a game name (like `HADLEY`) and press Play. If that
game is running you join it, on its map; if not, you start it. Tell your friends the name or share
the link. There is no game server. If everyone has gone down the mines, it offers to take you after
them.

Joining a running game, you wait on the menu until you've linked up with everyone ("Connecting to 2
of 3 players…"), or 15 seconds. If a link drops, the two of you find each other again within a few
seconds. A notice pops up when someone joins or leaves, and the league table (hold Tab) shows the
game's name, map and players.

Everyone in a game plays the same version. A page left open since the game last changed updates
itself when you press Play. Joining a newer game, you're asked to refresh; joining an older one,
you're told to wait while they do, and they get a banner saying so.

**Voice chat**: your mic is open to players nearby, heard in 3D and fading out by 60 m. Hold V
(Radio) to talk to the whole room through a crackly walkie-talkie. The browser asks for the mic
when you press Play, and you can mute it from the pause menu.

Other players wear a jacket in their own colour, with their own face and hair. Their heads turn to
look where they're looking, their legs walk, run and crouch, and their mouths move as they talk.

## Look and sound

A modern take on 90s Build-engine shooters: chunky pixel-art textures drawn in the browser, leafy
swaying trees that become flat pictures facing you further off, Duke Nukem style, tufts of grass
with buttercups and daisies, and a punchy colour grade with a fine dither, drawn at about 540 lines
and scaled up in crisp square pixels. The sound effects and the soundtrack (procedural 90s-shooter
metal) are synthesised in the browser, so the game ships no audio files. The soundtrack follows the
game's clock: besides the theme there is a track for each part of the day, from First Light on
Solsbury Hill at dawn through School Run Stampede, Elevenses at the Deli, High Noon on North Road,
Three O'Clock Lull, Golden Hour, Rainbow Wood, The Lamps Come On and Last Orders to Witching Hour
on the Skyline and Foxes at Four, and each is much the likeliest to come on round its own hour (the
others still turn up now and then, and you can skip to another whenever you like). Under it all is the sound of
where you are, anywhere in Bath: a far-off murmur of traffic in town, birdsong in the woods and gardens
(sparrows, a blackbird, wood pigeons, crows), the wind gusting across open ground and up on the roofs,
a skylark over the fields, and by night a quieter town and the odd owl. It goes muffled indoors and in a car.
